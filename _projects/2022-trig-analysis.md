In this fluid mechanics dissection, I analyzed the internal airflow behavior of a household hair dryer by focusing on key components such as the axial fan and the nozzle geometry. The fan was examined in terms of how it induces airflow and increases the velocity of air through the device, while the nozzle shape was analyzed for its role in accelerating the flow and directing it toward the outlet. Fundamental mechanical principles, including conservation of mass and Bernoulli’s equation, were applied to explain how changes in cross-sectional area affect air velocity and pressure throughout the system. By relating these theoretical concepts to the physical design of the hair dryer, this dissection provided insight into how relatively simple fluid mechanical principles are used to achieve efficient airflow and effective heat transfer in everyday consumer devices.
